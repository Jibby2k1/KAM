# Temporal addresses with censored durations

When some durations are visible only as intervals, the best address score depends on how the question time was chosen. At 64 events with 35% of durations bucketed into 30-minute intervals, a clock-time model recovered 74.64% of clock-sampled targets and an explicit event-sampling model recovered 67.77% of event-sampled targets. Swapping the models between the two question sources reduced exact recovery. On 1,173 authored counterfactual pairs, the event-sampling model changed full-coverage accuracy little and improved selective recovery at 80% retained coverage.

## Derivation and controls

Every query received the anchor, an elapsed time in minutes, and the event sequence. A duration was either exact or replaced with a known inclusive interval. Masks were drawn independently and reused across the original, sham, and intervention for each authored schedule. The duration oracle scored the uncorrupted schedule at the same elapsed time. The no-censoring control recovered every generated and authored answer.

At candidate event $j$, let $S_j$ be its start relative to the anchor, $D_j$ its duration, and $q$ the question time. The clock-time address score is $P(S_j\le q) - P(S_j+D_j\le q)$. Subtracting the visible lower bounds leaves a sum of independent uniform integer residuals. Caching its cumulative distribution by the count of censored durations allows both endpoint probabilities to be looked up without enumerating full schedules. The event-sampling score averages $\mathbf{1}[S_j\le q<S_j+D_j]/D_j$ and normalizes across candidates. The reciprocal corrects for drawing an event uniformly and then a minute within it. Exhaustive enumeration on a small schedule verifies both computations. A continuity-corrected Gaussian approximates the clock-time rule using the same means and variances. The midpoint selects the event by accumulating interval centers.

Three independent seed panels used 2,048 generated questions per length and question-time convention. Clock samples were drawn uniformly within the observed lower bound on the remaining span, making the time independent of the hidden residuals conditional on the observations. Event samples were generated by drawing an event uniformly from the anchor onward, then drawing a minute in it. These synthetic conventions use known generators. The authored set received simulated censoring on top of verified typed schedules. ## Exact recovery under 35% censoring with 30-minute bins

| Events | Uniform clock midpoint | Clock Gaussian | Exact clock score | Uniform event midpoint | Clock score on event queries | Event score |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 87.17% | 87.37% | 87.35% | 77.75% | 75.94% | 82.96% |
| 64 | 73.71% | 74.56% | 74.64% | 64.31% | 61.34% | 67.77% |
| 256 | 57.65% | 59.59% | 59.59% | 45.15% | 43.13% | 47.54% |
| 1,024 | 39.65% | 42.94% | 42.97% | 30.53% | 28.53% | 30.97% |

The event rule gained 3.08 to 3.66 percentage points versus midpoint across the three 64-event panels. Its gains narrowed to 0.15 to 0.68 points at 1,024 events and each per-seed paired interval included zero. On uniform-clock queries Gaussian and exact clock scores differed by at most 0.08 points in the table. On 1,024-event clock queries the event rule fell to 34.46%, versus 42.97% with the matched clock rule. The method choice can matter more than the numerical approximation.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| 35% censored, 30-minute bins | 42.37% | 42.91% | 44.21% | 47.16% |
| 65% censored, 30-minute bins | 31.54% | 32.08% | 32.76% | 35.00% |

Pair confidence for abstention is the smaller of the two individual address scores. The 80% operating point ranks each seed's pairs by confidence, with fixed random tie-breaking. It describes matched retained coverage on these panels. For 35% censoring and 30-minute bins, the unconditional event-rule improvement was 0.34 to 0.85 points across three masks on the same authored set. All corresponding question-bootstrap intervals included zero. The event rule changed 1.65% of sham choices at that setting versus 0.26% for midpoint. Gaussian and exact clock scores yielded 38.96% and 38.53% correct on both authored variants. The experiment separates uncertainty propagation and assumptions about the query from the existing typed-anchor extraction and learned routing experiments.
